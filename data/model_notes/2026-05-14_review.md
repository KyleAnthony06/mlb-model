## What the model got right

- Smaller NRFI cards continue to work better than forced volume. The May 14 NRFI card went 2-1.
- Cold/wind-in environments were useful for first-inning run suppression: PHI @ BOS and CHC @ ATL both hit.
- Elite whiff/workload overs still grade well: Chris Sale cleared 6.5 K with 8 K.
- Low-line overs with a real workload path can work: Ben Brown cleared 3.5 K with 7 K.

## What the model got wrong

- KC @ CWS NRFI missed badly with 3 first-inning runs. The model continued to underrate top-order damage in this matchup family.
- Kris Bubic over 5.5 K missed with 4 K, showing that opponent K path is not enough if game flow/contact suppresses strikeout volume.
- Anthony Kay under 3.5 K lost by the hook. Very low K unders have poor margin for error.
- Landen Roupp under 5.5 K lost with 7 K, showing that under bets against starters with normal leash can fail even against strong lineups.

## Repeatable lessons for the next slate

1. NRFI remains the stronger model sleeve than props right now. Keep NRFI selective and environment-driven.
2. Avoid low-margin K unders at 3.5 unless there is a clear opener/pitch-count cap or extremely low strikeout profile.
3. Prefer K overs on elite arms or low lines with stable workload; avoid mid-line overs that rely mostly on opponent strikeout reputation.
4. Do not keep attacking the same volatile matchup bucket after it has burned multiple times; KC/CWS-type top-order volatility needs a penalty.
5. High-total, heat, Coors, Sutter, wind-out, and fragile starters should remain hard NRFI passes.

## Rule changes for next model version

- Prop tightening: K props need either elite pitcher skill or a discounted line with workload proof. Middle-tier overs are downgraded.
- Under tightening: K unders below 4.5 require a pitch-count/opener edge; avoid under 3.5 unless the pitcher is very unlikely to reach 5 innings.
- Volatile-top-order penalty: downgrade NRFI in games with repeated first-inning scoring or multiple top-three OBP/power threats on both sides.
- Maintain smaller cards and no forced Top 10.
