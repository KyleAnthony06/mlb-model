## What the model got wrong

- The model overweighted one elite starter in NRFIs where the other starter had to face a dangerous home top order. COL @ PIT and SF @ LAD both failed because the non-elite side of the pitching matchup allowed a first-inning run.
- The model treated a roof as weather-risk removal but did not penalize enough for the park/offensive context. ARI @ TEX lost despite the closed-roof setup.
- The model included PHI @ BOS in the Top 10 even though its own reasoning identified Moran vs Turner/Schwarber/Harper at Fenway as a major YRFI path.
- Tatsuya Imai over 4.5 K lost because the model did not penalize enough for limited MLB sample and leash uncertainty; he recorded only 3 K in 4.0 IP.
- Yoshinobu Yamamoto under 6.5 K lost because elite whiff stuff and normal leash beat the contact-oriented top-order argument; he recorded 8 K.

## Repeatable lessons for the next slate

1. Do not let one elite starter carry an NRFI grade by himself. If the other starter is low-sample, below average, or facing a powerful top three, cap the NRFI score unless both first-inning matchups are favorable.
2. Treat roof status as neutral weather control, not as a run-prevention boost. Park factor and top-order quality still drive scoring risk.
3. Remove or sharply downgrade NRFIs when the written rationale contains a clear first-inning failure path, especially a weak/low-sample starter against multiple high-ISO top-three hitters.
4. K overs remain the better first pass than K unders when the line is reasonable. A high-quality pitcher with a normal workload can beat a contact-matchup under.
5. Limited-MLB-sample pitchers need a leash penalty for K overs. Require stronger projected outs/innings support before betting over.

## Rule changes for next model version

- NRFI asymmetry cap: if only one starter is clearly NRFI-positive and the other faces at least two top-three bats with strong OBP/power indicators, cap confidence at 58% before weather/park adjustments.
- Roof adjustment: roof can remove wind/temperature volatility but cannot add confidence by itself. Apply park factor separately.
- Fragility filter: if a Top 10 NRFI rationale includes a phrase equivalent to "major YRFI threat," either remove it from the Top 10 or cap it at 51-52%.
- K-over leash filter: for pitchers with limited MLB sample, require projected workload of at least 5 innings or a clearly discounted line before recommending an over.
- K-under restraint: avoid unders against elite whiff pitchers unless there is a second independent edge such as pitch-count limit, injury return, bad weather delay risk, or a full-lineup low-K profile.
