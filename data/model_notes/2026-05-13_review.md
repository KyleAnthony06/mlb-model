# 2026-05-13 Post-Slate Review

## Final record

- Overall: 6-4, 60.0%
- NRFI: 3-2, 60.0%
- Pitcher props: 3-2, 60.0%
- Two-day aggregate after May 13: 15-10, 60.0%

## What the model got right

- Selective NRFI sizing helped. The card was cut to five NRFIs instead of forcing a full Top 10, and it still finished 3-2.
- The model correctly kept TB @ TOR and KC @ CWS as modest-confidence NRFIs; both hit.
- The model correctly identified K-over value on Christian Scott, Dylan Cease, and Max Meyer.
- The v2 rule to avoid casual K unders against strong whiff pitchers was useful; no K unders were added to the card.

## What the model got wrong

- LAA @ CLE lost because Cleveland scored 2 in the first. The model underestimated top-three/first-inning volatility against Reid Detmers.
- DET @ NYM lost because Detroit scored 2 in the first. The model overweighted park context and did not penalize the Detroit top-three form enough after that lineup had shown immediate run creation risk.
- Mitch Keller over 4.5 K lost with only 3 K. The low line was not enough because game flow/contact efficiency shortened the K path.
- Max Fried over 5.5 K lost badly with only 2 K. This reinforced that K props need workload/game-state protection, not just matchup strikeout pockets.

## Repeatable lessons for the next slate

1. Treat recent first-inning damage from the same lineup as a short-term warning, especially when the top three are unchanged or improved.
2. Do not assume low K lines are safe if the opposing lineup can create quick balls in play and shorten counts.
3. For K overs, require both a whiff matchup and a plausible 90+ pitch / 5+ inning workload path.
4. Be willing to use K unders against non-elite arms facing contact-heavy or elite offenses; the v2 warning was mainly about avoiding unders against elite whiff pitchers.
5. Continue making smaller cards when the slate quality is thin.

## Rule changes for next model version

- Recent-lineup-warning penalty: downgrade NRFI confidence if the opposing top three scored in the first inning yesterday and remains materially similar.
- K-over workload gate: do not recommend K overs unless workload and count efficiency can realistically support the line.
- K-under reintroduction: unders are allowed against non-elite pitchers when the line is high relative to skill/workload or the opponent has strong contact quality.
- Late-slate discipline: exclude already-started games and avoid forcing picks from high-total/high-risk environments.
